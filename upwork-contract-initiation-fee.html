Upwork Contract Initiation Fee ($0.99 to $14.99): Who Pays?

The contract initiation fee is paid by the client, once per new contract, and ranges from $0.99 to $14.99. It is separate from the freelancer service fee and is not deducted from what a freelancer earns. Freelancers still care, because it adds to what a client pays and can make small jobs less attractive to hire.

What it means for you as a freelancer

  • Your service fee (0-15%) is a separate deduction; see the Upwork service fee explained.
  • Clients hiring for tiny one-off jobs pay proportionally more in fees, so bundling small tasks into one fixed-price contract can lower their cost.
  • Do not subtract this fee from your own net. Your payout is affected by your service fee and withdrawal fee only; see Upwork withdrawal fees outside the US.

FAQ

Who pays the Upwork contract initiation fee?

Clients. Upwork's help page says it is a one-time fee clients pay on each new Marketplace or Project Catalog contract, from $0.99 to $14.99. It is not deducted from the freelancer's earnings.

When is it charged?

For hourly contracts at the client's first payment to the freelancer; for fixed-price contracts when the client funds the contract or its first milestone. Adding milestones to an existing fixed-price contract does not trigger a new fee.

Is it refundable?

Generally not once the client has paid the freelancer, even if the freelancer refunds the client or the client wins a dispute. For fixed-price contracts the fee is returned if the milestone is never released.

Does hiring the same freelancer again cost another fee?

Yes. Each new contract carries its own fee, including a contract with a freelancer the client has hired before.
