PayPal Currency Conversion Fee (4%) Explained

When PayPal converts money between currencies, the exchange rate it gives you includes a spread: 4.00% in three named cases and 3.00% for all other transactions, or another amount disclosed during the transaction. On $1,000 converted that is $40 or $30, on top of the normal PayPal fee.

Last checked: 2026-11-23 against PayPal's US page, linked below. Anything we could not confirm is marked unverified.

What the spread is

A spread is a markup built into the exchange rate. You will not see a line called "conversion fee"; instead the rate you get is worse than the market rate by about 3% or 4%. It only exists when money changes currency, for example when a USD payment lands in a EUR balance.

How to reduce it

  • Ask the client to pay in the currency of your balance, so no conversion happens.
  • Check the rate on the confirmation screen before accepting a conversion.
  • Compare it with your bank's rate; we did not verify any bank's terms.

FAQ

What is PayPal's 4% currency conversion fee?

PayPal's US page says the exchange rate PayPal gives you includes a currency conversion spread. It is 4.00% when you pay for goods or services priced in another currency, send money to friends or family who receive another currency, or send Payouts in another currency, and 3.00% for all other transactions, or such other amount as is disclosed during the transaction.

Is the 4% charged on top of the normal PayPal fee?

Yes. It is a separate cost, built into the exchange rate rather than shown as a fee line. It only applies if PayPal converts money from one currency to another.

Does it apply when a client pays me in the currency of my PayPal balance?

No conversion happens, so no spread applies. A spread appears only when the currency changes, for example when a USD payment is converted into a EUR balance.

Which rate applies when I receive a client payment and convert it?

The page lists 4.00% for three named cases (paying, friends and family, Payouts) and 3.00% for all other transactions. Which line covers converting money you receive as a seller is not spelled out in the part we read, so treat 3% to 4% as the range and check the rate PayPal shows before you confirm.

How can I avoid the conversion spread?

Hold a balance in the currency you are paid in, and ask clients to pay in it. Withdrawing to a bank in that currency, if your bank accepts it, avoids a PayPal conversion, but your bank may charge its own fees. We did not verify any bank's terms.
