How it is calculated

Upwork takes a percentage of each payment (the freelancer service fee), then you pay a flat fee each time you withdraw.

Net = payment x (1 - fee%) - withdrawal fee.
What to charge = (target net + withdrawal fee) / (1 - fee%).

Example: a $500 payment at 10% leaves $450; a $0.99 local bank withdrawal leaves $449.01. To keep $449.01 you charge $500. Your bank may add its own incoming fees or exchange spread, which is not included. The client's marketplace fee is paid by the client on top and is not part of your net.

FAQ

What is Upwork's freelancer service fee?

Upwork says the fee varies per contract and is locked once the contract starts. A range of 0-15% is reported on an Upwork page excerpt; the old flat 10% from 2023 is no longer the stated rule. Check the rate on your proposal.

How much does it cost to withdraw from Upwork?

Direct to local bank (non-US) is $0.99 per withdrawal, a USD wire is $50, and Direct to U.S. bank is $2.99 if your tax address is outside the US (from 2026-09-01). Your bank may charge extra.

How do I price a job so I keep a specific amount?

Switch the calculator to 'I want to receive X'. It divides by (1 - fee%) after adding the withdrawal fee, so the result nets exactly your target.

Does the client fee reduce my earnings?

No, the client marketplace fee is charged to the client on top of your price. We could not confirm its current rate from an official page, so it is not used here.

Are PayPal withdrawals included?

No. We could not confirm the current PayPal withdrawal fee from an official Upwork page, so it is marked unverified and left out.
